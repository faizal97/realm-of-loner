Realm of Loner: privacy

Last updated 12 October 2026. Applies to the game in a browser (faizal97.github.io/realm-of-loner) and the Android app.

In short: Realm of Loner has no analytics and needs no account. Your characters are saved on your own device. Two things are optional and off until you turn them on: cloud save puts a copy of your characters in a hidden folder in your own Google Drive, and Friends shares what you choose of your characters with the players you accept as friends, through Google's Firebase service. We never receive your saves.

What the game keeps, and where

  • Your characters and settings are saved on your device: in your browser's storage on the web, and in the app's own storage on Android. They stay there until you delete them, clear your browser's site data, or uninstall the app.
  • Everyone else on the realm is simulated by the game on your device. There are no other real players, and nothing you do in the game is sent anywhere.

Cloud save (optional)

In Settings → Cloud save you can sign in with Google to keep a copy of your characters in your Google Drive. If you never sign in, the game never contacts Google.

  • What the game can see: only its own hidden app folder in your Drive (the permission Google shows as "See, create and delete its own configuration data in your Google Drive"). It cannot see, open or change any of your other files.
  • What it stores there: one file per character, holding that character's save, plus a few labels used to show and compare saves: the character's name, level, XP, class, race and look, total play time, when it was saved, whether it was saved from the app or a browser, and a revision number. Since version 10.1.1 there is also one small file shared by all your characters: your Mentor Marks, the heirlooms you have unlocked, and which story scenes, Lore Journal pages and tips you have already seen.
  • Who receives it: nobody but you and Google, which stores the files in your Drive under Google's privacy policy. The files go straight from your device to your Drive; there is no server of ours in between. The game does not ask for your email address or profile.
  • Sign-in: in a browser, Google's sign-in script is loaded from accounts.google.com only when you open Cloud save or are signed in, and the short-lived access it gives is kept in memory only. In the Android app, sign-in is handled by Google Play services on your phone.
  • The game's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements: your data is used only to back up and restore your characters, and is never sold, shared or used for ads.

Friends (optional)

In Social → Friends you can add real players by friend code. If you never turn Friends on, nothing about you goes to Firebase.

  • Sign-in: Friends uses your Google sign-in, asking only for your Google account's ID (the permission Google calls "openid"), not your email address, name or photo. Firebase keeps a sign-in record with that ID.
  • What your friends see: for each character you share (all of them, unless you switch one off in Friends → My sharing): its name, race, class, look, level, role, talents, professions and skill, guild name and rank, title, number of mounts and the gear it wears. Also which shared character you are playing and where (zone, place or dungeon), whether you are online, and when you last played.
  • What else is stored: your friend code; your friend list (Firebase IDs and the date you became friends); friend requests (the name, class and level of the character a request was sent from); and, while the game is open, one online entry per open game, with the list of friends allowed to see it.
  • Where: on Google's Firebase servers, in Jakarta (Cloud Firestore) and Singapore (Realtime Database), under Firebase's privacy and security terms.
  • Who can see it: only the players you have accepted as friends. The rules on Firebase refuse everyone else. As the owner of the game's Firebase project, the developer can also open the database; it is not used for anything but running Friends, and it is never sold or shared.
  • Firebase's code is loaded from Google (www.gstatic.com) only when you open Friends or have it on.

Guides you send (optional)

On the guide site you can write a guide and send it for review. Writing and previewing stay in your browser; only sending uses Firebase.

  • Sign-in: the same Google sign-in as Friends, asking only for your Google account's ID.
  • What is stored: your Firebase ID, the class, title, the name you choose to show and the guide's text, when you sent it, and whether it was approved (with the reason, if it was not). One guide per account; sending again replaces it.
  • Who can see it: you and the developer, until it is approved. An approved guide is public: its title, your chosen name and its text go on the guide site and into the game's public code repository.
  • Removing it: on the guide site's Write page, Delete what you sent (until it is approved). To take down a published guide, ask on Discord.

Removing your cloud data

  • One character: in the game, Delete character and tick "Also delete the copy in Google Drive".
  • Everything: in Google Drive on the web, open Settings → Manage apps, find Realm of Loner, and choose Options → Delete hidden app data.
  • Friends, turned off: in Social → Friends, Turn off Friends deletes your profile, online status and the requests you sent. Your friend list and code are kept (they point only to your Firebase ID), so turning it on again restores your friends.
  • Friends, everything: Delete my Friends data also deletes your code and every friendship, on both sides.
  • Take away the game's access: in your Google Account, open Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services, choose Realm of Loner, and remove its access. Signing out in the game only signs out that one device.

Other connections the game makes

  • Firebase (only with Friends on): see Friends above.
  • Update checks: the game asks GitHub (api.github.com) whether a newer version has been released. This is a plain request with nothing about you or your game in it; GitHub, like any website, sees your IP address.
  • Hosting: the web version is served by GitHub Pages. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Bug reports: only if you choose to send one. The report is shown to you first and holds the game version, your device and browser type, and what you were doing in the game (never your save). You send it yourself, as a GitHub issue or by pasting it on Discord.
  • Links: Discord, Ko-fi and SociaBuzz open in your browser only when you tap them. Those are other websites with their own privacy policies; the game sends them nothing.

Changes and contact

If what the game does with data changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. Questions: ask on Discord or open an issue on GitHub.
